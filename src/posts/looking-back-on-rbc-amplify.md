## Starting With More Questions Than Answers

RBC Amplify was, essentially, a four-month hackathon. You are placed on a small team, given a challenge from a line of business within RBC, and asked to come up with a solution to solve it.

Going in, I honestly did not know exactly what I was getting myself into. I was applying broadly, and I knew Amplify would be more freeform and creative than a typical internship, but I did not fully understand what that would mean until day one.

Walking into the opening event was a little anxiety-building. Outside of a couple of people I had met at the open house, I knew basically no one. I remember sitting at the group tables, getting our Amplify merch, and waiting anxiously to open the envelopes that very explicitly said 'DO NOT OPEN'.

About halfway through the day, we were finally allowed to open our envelopes.

That was when we learned what would shape the next four months. Even then, there was a lot of ambiguity. The challenge statement was complex, outside of our existing background, and not something any of us immediately knew how to tackle. In hindsight, that was probably the point.

## Building Phoenix

Teams in Amplify are supposed to be four people, but we were a team of three: Ryan Fang, our business analyst; Jessica Wang, our software developer; and me, as the data engineer.

Our challenge was to explore how modern technology could help make foreign-exchange transactions cheaper, more transparent, and more efficient for RBC’s global clients.

Because of NDA and patent considerations, I cannot get into the full details of what we built. At a high level, though, Phoenix explored a blockchain-based approach to modernizing payment rails for foreign exchange. The goal was to make transactions faster and lower-cost while fitting within the realities of RBC Capital Markets and its existing business model.

Although I started out focused on data engineering, I ended up taking on a broader software-engineering role. I worked across the front end, back end, infrastructure, and internal connections that helped bring the product together. That isn't to say that my team wasn't helping out majorly in these sections either, but I did try to take the lead on them as my teammates were stronger in other areas that needed completion throughout the summer.

## Learning to Pivot

One of the most important moments came when we met with the foreign-exchange desk in Capital Markets to pitch our solution.

At the time, we had built out our own approach to pricing. What we learned was that the desk already handled pricing internally, and our idea risked overlapping with the way they operated. We were not completely shut down, but we definitely had to reconsider and pivot.

That meeting gave us a much-needed new perspective. We had been approaching the problem from a very tech-heavy angle, and it showed us that we had not considered every business perspective we needed to, something that was greatly expressed in the design thinking training workshops in the first two weeks of the program...

So we went back to the drawing board. We asked more questions, spoke with more people, and sought out expertise from people who understood the field far better than we did. It was a good reminder that you cannot just build blindly based on what you think people will want. You have to actually listen.

## The Work Behind the Work

Blockchain was a major learning curve. None of us had much practical experience beyond general familiarity with things like Bitcoin or Ethereum. To think about how it could be used in a bank, we had to understand it at a much deeper level.

It was a ton of information, but I really enjoyed that part. I love learning, and Amplify gave us the space to work through something unfamiliar and figure out how it could apply in a real-world setting. We were given the tools and freedom to explore, build, break, and then build again.

We also had to figure out the identity of our solution. Jessica was incredibly talented on the design side, and with support from RBCx and our mentors, we iterated until Phoenix had a brand that felt like our own. The design, slides, and UI became something people noticed, especially during the final presentations, so hats off to Jessica on that.

And, of course, there was the challenge of being one person short. As AmpExpo got closer, we had to account for the fact that we were doing a four-person project with three people. There were some late nights and a lot of work, but we made it work.

## What I Learned

One of the biggest things I learned was the value of building early.

In a fast-moving, startup-like environment, it was much more useful to show mentors, clients, and stakeholders something tangible than to stay on a drawing board forever. Once people could see what we were thinking, they could react to it, challenge it, and help us improve it.

I also learned how important constant communication is in a small team. You have to be on the same page, stay up to speed on what everyone else is doing, and be willing to ask questions. Otherwise, things are missed and time is wasted.

The other big lesson was learning to reach out. Amplify showed me how much value there is in talking to people with more experience, asking for their perspective, and building relationships. There is a huge network of people at RBC who are willing to help if you take the initiative to ask.

## A Different Kind of Internship

Before Amplify, I think I had the same assumptions many people have about working at a bank: that it would be restrictive, slow-moving, and very structured.

Amplify completely challenged that.

It was fast-paced, freeform, and genuinely entrepreneurial. We had guidance from mentors, clients, and stakeholders, but we had real ownership over what we built, how we built it, and how we pitched it. We managed our own deadlines, reports, and submissions.

It was in fact "not your typical internship", as the program team regularly states, and I think that is exactly what made it so special.

## AmpExpo

AmpExpo was the culmination of the whole summer.

Making it through the first round was already exciting. Reaching the finals and pitching Phoenix in front of a massive auditorium of senior RBC leaders was unreal. We were able to answer questions and have real conversations with people across Capital Markets, AI, Technology, and Wealth Management. The entire room had been converted from three small auditoriums to one that housed probably well over 2000 people during the final presentations. It was something none of us had expected, at all.

Then we won the Industry Disruptor Award, along with the $20,000 prize.

That moment was crazy. I was proud of the result, but I was also proud of how our three-person team made it through the summer together. The wrap-up was exciting and a little bittersweet. It was amazing to see what everyone had built, but it also meant the summer and the time with all of those people was coming to an end.

## The People Made It

The people were probably the biggest highlight.

Everyone was incredibly friendly and talented. Meeting people across the cohort, hearing about their goals, and seeing how hard they were working pushed me to push myself further too.

Amplify really showed me what it means to surround yourself with people who make you better. The mentors knew where challenges would come up and helped us get back on track when things got difficult. Ryan brought strong business thinking to the team, and Jessica’s design work helped Phoenix stand out.

I also came out of the summer more comfortable meeting new people and breaking out of my shell, something that I wanted to achieve in my Letter to My Future Self at the beginning of the summer. I hope to stay in touch with many of the people I met and see where they end up, because I genuinely think they all have incredibly bright futures.

## Looking Forward

I am carrying a lot forward from Amplify: curiosity, creativity, comfort with ambiguity, and the confidence to take a shot at questions that have not been answered before.

Most of all, I am carrying forward the habit of reaching out to people. Building a network, learning from people with more experience, and staying open to conversations can have a huge impact on where you end up.

I would recommend Amplify to anyone who wants to try something new in tech, build something from the ground up, and have real ownership over their work. It was challenging, fast-moving, and at times nerve-wracking—but it was also one of the most rewarding experiences I have had.
